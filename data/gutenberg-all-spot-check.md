## Read by hand (2026-10-27)

- The two failures are pen names: Gutenberg's page says "Edna Lyall" and "Artemus Ward", and the rows credit the same people under their own names, Ada Ellen Bayly and Charles Farrar Browne, as Wikidata does. Both are right.
- This sample is the second of the day. The author check against each book's own page is new, added after an earlier version of these files was found to rename about 1,600 authors: a person was respelt as any catalog author with the same initial and a near surname (Jane Austen as "Jane G. Austin", Isabella Beeton as "Isaac Newton"). The respelling now needs the same first name, a surname one letter away and the same death year, and every row's author was compared with Gutenberg's: none differ except for the same person named by Wikidata (John Dalberg-Acton for Lord Acton).
- Fixed at the same time:
  - single volumes whose complete edition is in the catalog under another title (*Oliver Twist*, Vol. 1–3);
  - Wikidata titles that named a different work (*Il Penseroso* for Milton's four poems) or the original's title (*La Débâcle* for *The Downfall*); titles are now Gutenberg's;
  - links that would have been added to the wrong books: Haggard's *Cleopatra* to নবীনচন্দ্র সেন's poem, an English translation to the Bangla *নীল দর্পণ*, one volume to a whole book;
  - Wikidata names that were another person or a family (William Salesbury as a Marquess of Salisbury, "Young baronets of Dominica");
  - peerage numbers in names ("1st Baron"), which put Latin digits into descriptions.
- Death years, checked for all 35,136 rows on 2026-10-28. The author renaming above changed names only: between the files of 485827e and these, 2,446 names changed and no death year did. One thing did need fixing. A death year came from Wikidata's item for the Wikipedia page Gutenberg gives a person, whoever that item was, so Jules Remy's rights note gave Oscar Straus's death (1926); it now gives his own (1893). Wikidata's year and name are now used only when its item is that person (`wikidataIsPerson`). For the 1965 rule, a later year from any item still counts, since it can only leave a book out, and someone Gutenberg gives no year passes only on their own item's. Every row's death year is its latest author's, and every author, translator and editor died in 1965 or earlier on those years. No row was dropped or added; two changed (Remy's note, and David M. De Witt now named David Miller DeWitt).
